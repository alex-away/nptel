## jargon buster

- **oecd (organisation for economic co-operation and development):** an international organization that develops policies and standards, including key principles for trustworthy ai.
- **nist (national institute of standards and technology):** a u.s. agency that develops standards and is hosting the u.s. ai safety institute.
- **meity (ministry of electronics and information technology):** an indian government ministry involved in ai policy and regulation.

---

## approaches to ai governance

- **controlling ai risks:** governance can be approached in three ways, similar to traditional cybersecurity:
    1.  **train the users:** using faqs and documentation to educate people.
    2.  **user interface (ui) solutions:** providing warnings directly in the user interface (e.g., "chats may be reviewed").
    3.  **silently eliminating the threat:** this is where high-level **regulation and governance** fit. they work in the background to enforce safety, much like a spam filter.

---

## key policies and principles

- **oecd ai principles:** a foundational framework for global ai governance. key principles include:
    - inclusive growth and sustainability.
    - human rights and democratic values.
    - **transparency and explainability.**
    - **robustness, security, and safety.**
    - **accountability.**
    - building human capacity and fostering international cooperation.
- **the eu ai act:** a landmark law that uses a **risk-based approach** to regulate ai.
    - **unacceptable risk (banned):** systems that are a clear threat to people, such as **social scoring** or manipulative ai that targets vulnerable groups.
    - **high-risk:** systems used in critical areas like employment, law enforcement, and medical devices. these face strict requirements for transparency, oversight, and registration.
    - **transparency rules:** requires that ai-generated content (deepfakes) be clearly labeled.

## the future of ai

- **global ai safety institutes:** countries around the world (including the uk, us, and others) are establishing national **ai safety institutes** to research and evaluate ai risks and inform policy.
- **india's ai mission:** ai policy in india is handled by the national ai mission and **meity**, with ongoing discussions about creating a dedicated ai safety institute.
- **critical governance questions:**
    - can a single policy work across different sectors, geographies, and tasks?
    - should we govern the **ai technology itself** or just its **applications**?
